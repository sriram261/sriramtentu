From a thesis on stuttering to companies on two continents

Oct 15, 2026 · 5 min read

I landed in the United States in August 2022 with one plan: get the master's, get the job, build the life. Four years later I have the degree, the job, two companies, and seven publications. None of it went according to the original plan. Here is how it actually happened.

The arrival

I started my MS in Computer and Information Sciences at Oklahoma State University in the fall of 2022. I had done my undergrad in computer science at KL University in India, plus an internship at Tharas Tech and some pentesting work. But I arrived in Stillwater, Oklahoma with a very engineer-shaped hole in my plans: I did not know what my thesis would be about.

The thesis

That question got answered when I joined the stuttering research work at OSU. The lab was building virtual reality environments where people who stutter could practice speaking in realistic situations: ordering at a restaurant, talking to strangers. I ended up writing the code and doing the research, mentored by Dr. J. Cecil and Dr. John Tetnowski, with guidance from Dr. Ramesh Kaipa.

The main build was Bob's Burger Palace: a full 3D restaurant, HTC Vive and Oculus Quest, characters built with Reallusion Character Creator, animated with Mixamo. Participants showed a 45 percent improvement in speech fluency. It became my master's thesis, "Investigating the impact of virtual reality-based interactions among stutterers and non-stutterers," published by ProQuest in July 2024. The follow-up paper won the Best Outstanding Poster Award at IEEE SeGAH 2024 in Funchal, Portugal.

That thesis is still the most important thing I have ever built. Everything since has been a version of the same idea: software that helps real people do hard things.

The graduation and the in-between

I graduated in May 2024. Then came the in-between years, the ones that do not fit on a poster. I stayed on at OSU as a Unity 3D programmer for the lab, building virtual learning environments for speech therapy research. I started an MBA in entrepreneurship at KL University, which I finished in May 2026. And in June 2025 I joined Boeing as a Python developer, building automation and backend components across client projects until April 2026.

Each job taught me something the thesis did not: how production systems actually run, how clients actually think, and how slowly big organizations move. That last one stuck with me.

The first company: RamTech, October 2025

In October 2025 I founded RamTech Solutions LLC in Oklahoma City. The idea was simple: practical, scalable software for businesses that need more than a template. RamTech is how I sell websites and platforms to US clients today, including the $99 launch websites on this very site.

The second company: TenTwo, February 2026

But home was always part of the plan. In February 2026 I founded TenTwo Technologies Private Limited in India, based in Kaikaluru, Andhra Pradesh. TenTwo runs a paid internship program, sells Tuberose & Co. on Amazon India, and launched the Smiles initiative. Two companies, two continents, one person holding both threads.

The day job: Argencis, July 2026

In July 2026 I joined Argencis Medical as an IT Support Specialist. A durable medical equipment company, with a real platform that serves 400 plus clinics, and I ended up building its order tracking platform from scratch. That story has its own case study. Argencis is where I learned how healthcare software actually reaches patients, through clinics, referral sources, and one working dashboard at a time.

What is next

In June 2027 I move home to India to run TenTwo full time. RamTech keeps serving US clients from Oklahoma. The companies keep growing, the research continues, and this site keeps documenting all of it.

If there is a lesson, it is the boring one: pick real problems, ship real software, and do not ask for permission first. The thesis taught me the method. Everything after was practice.
