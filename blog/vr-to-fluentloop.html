From VR lab to browser: building FluentLoop

Oct 12, 2026 · 4 min read

For two years I worked in Oklahoma State University's stuttering research lab, building virtual reality environments where people who stutter could practice speaking. The research worked. In our studies, participants improved fluency by up to 45%. We published papers, won a best poster award at IEEE SeGAH 2024 in Portugal, presented at ASHA. On paper, a success story.

But there was a problem I couldn't stop thinking about: nobody owns a VR headset. The therapy worked in the lab and almost nowhere else. A treatment people can't access is barely a treatment at all.

The insight

The magic was never the headset. It was the method: structured practice, immediate feedback, gradual progression through difficulty levels. None of that requires virtual reality. A browser can do all of it. So I started rebuilding the core of the research as a web app any student could open on a phone.

That became FluentLoop. Open the browser, pick a level, record your voice, get AI-powered feedback in seconds. Speech scoring runs on Whisper, the same class of models behind modern voice AI. No installs, no headset, no appointment. Privacy-first: your audio stays in your browser.

Research to product is a translation job

The hardest part wasn't the code. It was translation. A research paper says "participants showed improved fluency under masked auditory feedback conditions." A product has to say "speak into your phone and I'll tell you how you did." Every clinical concept needed a plain-language, three-second interaction. That discipline, turning rigor into simplicity, is the real skill I took from the lab.

What is next

FluentLoop v2 is in progress: real user accounts, session history, and a proper level progression system. The research continues too, with ASHA 2025 follow-up work on stuttering and XR. The lab gave me the science. The browser gives it reach.
